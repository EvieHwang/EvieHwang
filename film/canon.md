# Film Canon
*Evie's personal film practice — an active-inference model of her taste*
*Last updated: 2026-06-29*

---

## How this file works

This is not a ranked watchlist. It is a polling instrument. Each film is a probe; each tier score is signal; the queue updates based on what the signal reveals about the underlying taste structure. The goal is to *discover* the shape of Evie's taste, not to confirm a prior guess about it.

**The loop, each round:**

1. Surface a **slate of 3–4 probes**. Every slate mixes *exploit* (high-confidence fits that sharpen a known axis) and *explore* (deliberate uncertainty and wildcards that prevent overfitting). Never zero explore.
2. Each probe carries an **explicit hypothesis**: the axis-uncertainty it tests, plus what a high tier versus a rejection would each reveal. A rejection is data, not a dead end.
3. Evie responds per film — **Reject** (premise wrong or won't watch; downweight that direction) or **Commit** (will watch).
4. After watching: **tier + note**. Update the relevant axis confidence in The Model below.
5. Every ~5–6 logged films: a **synthesis pass** — name the pattern the data now reveals, revise the open questions, re-balance the exploration.

**Hard pre-filter:** the Canon Criterion is a gate, not a variable. Nothing that fails it ever enters a slate, regardless of how well it fits the taste model.

**Conversation before an out-of-expectation grade.** If Evie moves to grade something in a way that conflicts with the model's expectation — an S for a film carrying a known conduct or content problem, or any tier that contradicts an established axis or the Canon Criterion — pause and surface it for discussion *before* recording the grade. Surprises get a conversation, not a silent entry. (See *The Shining* — the case that established this rule works as engagement, not reflex.)

## Canon Criterion

The ethical viewer rejects work with abusive content or abusive creators. Some judgment may be required; some work may be viewed as historical artifact. Where the actor is the victim, conscious viewing honors their talent despite the abuse.

Flattening female characters is treated as a *competence failure*, not merely an ethical blemish — it costs tiers, not just goodwill.

**Hard rule:** no S-tier for a film that was abusive to one of its female leads. On-set conduct counts, not only on-screen treatment. Craft can be S-level and the tier still capped by the conduct.

**On the hard rule — what it is and isn't.** It is not an aesthetic antipathy toward depictions of female suffering, applied reflexively. It is a commitment to *engage genuinely* with what's actually there. That means distinguishing harm imposed *on* an actor as a person (caps the tier) from harm an actor *summons as craft* in knowing partnership with a director whose intent is wholly instrumental to the work (does not). The test is the director's agenda and the lead's standing, not the presence of suffering. Where interpretations of consent genuinely cancel out, the person who was in the room holds the casting vote. (See *The Shining*: the edge case that proves rather than breaks the rule.)

Currently excluded: Polanski (convicted predator — cruelty *was* the point), von Trier (archetypal misogynist; female suffering as the auteur's signature). Under review: Besson (dropped allegation — question mark, not dismissal). Bertolucci not categorically excluded, but *Last Tango in Paris* is.

## The Model

### Established axes (high confidence — exploit territory)

- **Style as argument, not decoration.** Formal control that means something. Load-bearing across the entire watch log. *A→S distinguisher:* at S, the visual grammar is doing philosophical work — Tinker Tailor's grey stillness *is* the argument about what intelligence does to people; Sorrentino's lush decay *is* the argument about wasted life. At A, cinematography may be competent, purposeful, even evocative, but not load-bearing in this way. Visually interesting ≠ visually compelling. *Corollary confirmed by Arrival:* restraint without texture is not the same as load-bearing restraint. TTSS is restrained AND textured — every character actor playing it with ironic gravity, the grey saturated with meaning about the rottenness underneath. *Arrival*'s flat palette has no such irony; it lies there rather than argues.
- **Every affective register must earn its place through philosophical weight.** Mood, ennui, beauty, setting, atmosphere — none of these are ends in themselves. They must be in service of a thesis. *La Grande Bellezza* is about wasted life; *The Godfather* is about corruption; *Tinker Tailor* refuses emotion as an argument *about* that refusal. The sole exception: aesthetic and cultural texture at overwhelming intensity can substitute for explicit argument — but only when the intensity is extreme AND the viewer's cultural antenna connects. *In the Mood for Love* earns this exception (Hong Kong texture, Shanghainese-exile specificity, colour and music at full force). *Yi Yi* does not reach the threshold: the philosophy is real but quiet, and the cultural antenna doesn't connect. *Under the Skin* fails doubly — no argument, and nothing meaningful to say about Scotland even to someone who has lived there.
- **Clear-eyed protagonists who act anyway.** Clarity, not heroism. Leamas, Fowler, Clarice, Thomasin. The single most reliable predictor of a high score. The axis may be better understood as: clarity at the moment of decisive action, not throughout. Aesthetic corollary: the viewer needs enough of the person under the performance to feel the weight of their clarity when it arrives. *Refinement from Arrival:* the drama lives in the gap between what is seen and what must be accepted. The brutality is mundane; the horror belongs to the protagonist, not the world. Earning that acceptance must be visible on screen — the audience must watch the character work against the grain of their own understanding. When the protagonist arrives at clarity too gracefully, or when the performance fails to convey the weight of the knowing, the archetype doesn't fire. Louise Banks sees clearly but isn't shown earning it. Ryan Stone (Gravity) is the counter-example: shown working for every inch, the will to live emerges through the act of surviving rather than preceding it. *Inversion confirmed by The Zero Effect:* Zero is clear-eyed about everything except himself — the archetype flipped. His moment of clarity (letting Gloria go, choosing to remain what he is) arrives at the end and is no less earned for being self-directed. Competence and brokenness as the same wiring.
- **Female characters as competence test.** Evenhanded interiority earns; spending a woman as a plot hinge costs (cf. *Lives of Others* missing S on exactly this; *In the Mood for Love* earning S because opacity was applied to both leads). One licensed exception for *on-screen* treatment: diminishment that is *realistically earned within the story* rather than authorial (cf. *The Shining*, Wendy under terror). On-set conduct is governed by the hard rule, and adjudicated case by case — not assumed from the presence of suffering.
- **Cynicism without nihilism.** Cool that earns itself. *No Country*, *The Big Short*. Corollary confirmed by *The Witch*: "evil liberation" is legible and even sympathetic when the world that produced it is shown to be worse than the alternative. You don't have to endorse the choice to understand why it was made.
- **Competence as pleasure.** The satisfaction of watching skilled people be skilled. *Ocean's 8*, *The Martian*, *John Wick*. Extended form confirmed by *Bourne Ultimatum*: procedural intelligence as wit — Bourne anticipating CIA moves through inference rather than force is a specific pleasure distinct from physical competence, closer to the literary satisfaction of a perfect argument. *Deepened by The Zero Effect:* competence-as-pleasure works even when — perhaps especially when — the competence is inseparable from the protagonist's brokenness. Zero can only do what he does because he has severed himself from ordinary life. The pleasure is sharpened by the cost.
- **Warmth before loss (confirmed across registers).** Originally identified as a horror-specific rule: dread requires warmth that can be destroyed (*The Shining* S vs. *Under the Skin* D). Generalized after *A Most Wanted Man* (A): warmth-before-tragedy is a universal principle about emotional stakes. In any register where loss must cost, the viewer needs to know and care about the person before the machinery takes them.
- **Moral architecture over period aesthetic (confirmed).** The Cold War pull is structural, not era-specific. The search for this register extends beyond European mid-century settings.
- **Implication over observation (confirmed, with spectacle corollary).** Comedy creates distance; spectacle does the same. When any single dominant mode (comedy, action-as-spectacle) captures attention at the surface level, the philosophical register gets crowded out. B ceiling confirmed for both. Key distinction: *John Wick* (A) works because its argument is compact and delivered *by* the action — the choreography is characterological portraiture. *Bourne Ultimatum* (A) works the same way in the procedural register: the argument (intelligence apparatus vs. the man it made) is delivered through inference and anticipation, not statement. *Fury Road*'s argument requires a second level of attention the spectacle pulls focus from.
- **Durability / inexhaustibility as tier marker.** A film that gives a great time but leaves no residue belongs at B. A film that gives you its argument completely and perfectly in one sitting belongs at A — "already got the point." A film that stays open, creates the pull to return, and yields more on revisit belongs at S. The Shining's hotel geometry keeps working; Tinker Tailor gives you more when you know where it's going; The Godfather has layers that keep revealing themselves. *Confirmed distinction:* pleasurable durability (can rewatch many times at the same quality, for visual delight and storytelling pleasure — *Gravity*, *The Martian*, *Bourne Ultimatum*) is not the same as inexhaustibility (yields new things on return — S). Both are genuine high-value qualities; A is the right home for pleasurable durability. S requires discovery, not just love. "Unquotable" = B. "Already got the point" = A. "Creates the pull to return" = S.
- **Cultural antenna as amplifier.** Personal resonance with a film's landscape, culture, or specificity amplifies the signal. *In the Mood for Love*: Shanghainese-exile Hong Kong. *The Witch*: dark northern European/New England forest connected directly from lived experience (UK, Boston). When the antenna connects, the film's atmosphere lands in the body rather than being observed from outside. When it doesn't (*Yi Yi*), even a serious film can fail to land.
- **Country-of-origin neutral.** Vision is the filter, never the passport.
- **Structure performing thesis (candidate axis — unconfirmed).** *The Spy Who Came in from the Cold* performs the deception it depicts: viewer and Leamas are deceived simultaneously. When a film enacts its argument rather than states it, the craft register may be distinct from — and potentially higher than — the thesis alone suggests. *Strengthened by The Zero Effect:* Zero's method (the "Zero Effect" — looking for anything rather than something, becoming invisible to observe) is performed by the film's plot, not merely stated. The MacGuffin (missing car keys) reveals itself as beside the point; the film was looking for anything and found something real. Candidate for confirmation: *The Conformist*, *Oldboy*.
- **Sci-fi where the premise IS the argument (confirmed; epic spectacle is a separate, lower register).** Hard sci-fi reaches A when the sci-fi premise enacts rather than decorates the thesis: *Gravity* (A, survival as the will to live — survival and rediscovery enacted as the same action), *The Martian* (A, competence as human dignity), *Arrival* (A, linguistic determinism of time). Epic-scale space spectacle without a coherent delivered argument lands at B: *Prometheus* (B) and *Interstellar* (B) join *Fury Road* at the same ceiling — enjoyed from outside, no residue. The love for ships, aliens, and cosmic scale is real but is not itself load-bearing; it earns B, not A. Specific failure mode confirmed by *Prometheus*: one great image can give a film its entire emotional high point without the runtime earning it; the movie then exists only to frame that shot. Plot coherence is required for A: *Interstellar* breaks its own time mechanics where *Gravity* holds airtight. Future probes should target hard sci-fi with formal control, not epic spectacle.
- **Entertaining is not a disqualifier, but it is a ceiling marker.** Confirmed by *Michael Clayton* (A): when "entertaining is what it's for and what it's about," the ambition is the story rather than a philosophical or visual argument beyond it. Craft can be exceptional, moral architecture sharp, performance extraordinary — and the film still belongs at A rather than S. Pleasurably durable is not the same as inexhaustible.

### Open questions (low confidence — where the probes go)

1. **Does aesthetic achievement buy out narrative weakness, and above what threshold?** *In the Mood for Love* (S) forgave plotlessness because it was too beautiful to mind. The exchange rate between beauty and thesis is better understood now — beauty alone is insufficient, but beauty at overwhelming intensity with cultural antenna connected earns a specific exception.
2. **Le Carré vein: portrait complete.** Tinker Tailor (S), The Spy (A), AMWM (A), Quiet American (A). Pattern clear: consistently A or S; the gap between them is female-character integrity + visual grammar doing philosophical work + inexhaustibility. No further probes needed.
3. **Dry contemplative cinema: ANSWERED.** *Yi Yi* reject confirms: formal seriousness without a legible thesis doesn't land.
4. **Untested forms.** Pre-1960: ANSWERED (*Paths of Glory*, A). Comedy-as-craft: ANSWERED (B ceiling). Spectacle-as-primary: ANSWERED (*Fury Road*, B; confirmed by *Prometheus* B and *Interstellar* B). Animation and documentary still near-zero data.
5. **Anti-triumphalist / triumphalist hypothesis: ANSWERED.** *Fury Road* (B): spectacle mode prevented the argument from landing regardless of structural presence. *Arrival* (A): tainted triumph in a quiet philosophical register scores A — the argument arrived ("already got the point") but the emotional vehicle (Louise's acceptance of her daughter's death) failed to show its work. Confirmed: the anti-triumphalist pattern is not the source of high scores. Tainted triumph in the philosophical mode can reach A when the argument is received. The gap between A and S in this register requires the protagonist to be *shown earning* her clear-eyed acceptance — the drama of knowing, not just the knowledge.
6. **Dread register beyond Kubrick: PARTIALLY ANSWERED.** *The Witch* (A) confirms dread-as-architecture holds beyond Kubrick. But A, not S — the argument was complete in one viewing, not inexhaustible. S in the dread register requires architectural inexhaustibility: the horror keeps working on return because it's built into the film's geometry, not just its plot. *Hereditary* is still the live candidate — grief as horror, emotional stakes front-loaded. Question now: can Eggers or Aster achieve the inexhaustibility that separates Kubrick's dread from everyone else's?
