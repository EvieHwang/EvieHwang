## 1. Purpose — why I bother

**The altruistic core.** I believe housing, nutrition, healthcare, and basic education
are infrastructure — things a decent society guarantees as durable floors, not
discretionary benefits. Queer and trans Washingtonians, especially youth, are the
population for whom those floors are most contested right now. I have structural
policy conviction (regulatory floors over transfers, governance as integrity,
implementation over gesture) and a community that needs exactly that kind of
thinking applied on its behalf. The Commission is where my policy philosophy and my
people intersect.

**The honest selfish reasons — named because they're real and they're fine.**
- **Standing and voice.** A gubernatorial appointment gives me institutional standing
  to work on policy I'd otherwise only get to write essays about. It converts
  conviction into access.
- **Identity and integration.** This role lets my professional identity (systems,
  data, supply chains of policy) and my personal identity live in one place instead
  of parallel tracks. That integration is deeply satisfying.
- **Craft.** I like building durable institutional machinery — data governance
  architectures, accountability mechanisms, legible processes. The Commission is a
  venue to practice that craft on problems that matter.
- **Legacy on a clock.** The term is bounded — six years. A bounded term with a
  visible end concentrates the mind. I want to be able to point at specific,
  structural things that exist because I was here.

**The test I hold myself to:** at the end of the term, did I leave behind *structure*
— floors, data architecture, accountability mechanisms that survive a hostile
administration — or just participation?

## 2. Institutional context

**The Commission.** Created by SB 5356 (2019), codified at RCW 43.114. Advisory body:
15 gubernatorially appointed commissioners plus small staff, housed under the
Governor's office. Statutory role: improve the state's interface with 2SLGBTQIA+
Washingtonians, identify community needs, serve as a clearinghouse for state and
federal legislation. It does not provide direct services and does not take public
positions on bills — it advises the Governor, the legislature, and state agencies.
Every ambition below must be shaped to fit an *advisory* lever: what to track, whom
to convene, what to recommend, what data architecture to propose.

**Washington's structural floor (the terrain I defend and extend):**
- WLAD (RCW 49.60) — SOGI as protected classes; Human Rights Commission enforces.
- Shield law — gender-affirming and reproductive care protected; AG enforces.
- RCW 74.09.675 — Apple Health cannot categorically exclude gender-affirming care;
  HCA administers; OIC governs the private market.
- Conversion therapy = unprofessional conduct; DOH runs gender-marker
  self-attestation; OSPI governs gender-inclusive schools and athletics.

**Rules of the role.** All communications made or received in the conduct of
Commission business are subject to the Public Records Act. Working rule: this file
and its siblings are private personal thinking; anything lifted from them into a
communication with staff, commissioners, legislators, or agencies becomes a public
record when sent. (Confirm edge cases with Commission counsel.)

## 3. Six-year horizons to accomplish by end of second term

### H1 — SOGI data governance architecture exists
**Outcome:** Washington has a cross-agency SOGI data collection and governance
standard — modeled on Oregon's REALD+SOGI — adopted or formally in motion.

### H2 — Transgender youth protections survive contact with a hostile legal environment
**Outcome:** Whatever external threats emerge, Washington's protective architecture for queer and trans youth (shelter access, care access, school climate, athletics) has been stress-tested and reinforced, and has parity, i.e. a trans minor should have the same access, judged by the same neutral criteria, that any minor has. 

### H3 — The Commission itself runs better because I was on it
**Outcome:** the Commission's own working machinery — how it tracks legislation,
briefs commissioners, and institutionalizes knowledge across member turnover — is
more effective and durable.
