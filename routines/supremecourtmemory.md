# Supreme Court Memory File

> This file is maintained by the bi-weekly SCOTUS briefing system. It tracks deep dive topics already covered, significant cases from the current term, and long-arc precedent threads — so that each briefing builds on prior ones rather than repeating them.

---

## Purpose

Evie follows the Supreme Court as her primary news stream — because unlike most news, the Court's work is foundational rather than episodic. A case decided today may constrain legislation for a generation. Her orientation is that of a **liberally-inclined originalist**: accepting the originalist critique of judicial overreach while holding liberal political commitments, and believing the Court's legitimacy depends on its restraint, not its outcomes. The briefing serves both intellectual depth and practical awareness — particularly for her roles on the WA LGBTQ Commission and in civic tech policy.

---

## Briefing Format (Reference)

Each bi-weekly briefing contains:

1. **Status Snapshot** — Opinions released since last briefing; emergency applications and notable orders; argument calendar for the next two weeks
2. **Case Spotlight** — 1–2 pending cases examined in moderate depth (parties, question presented, circuit split or statutory hook, oral argument tea leaves if available)
3. **Deep Dive** — One topic per issue: a landmark precedent, a doctrinal thread, a constitutional concept, or a Court history item that broadens the context window for cases on the docket. These should NOT repeat. See tracking below.
4. **Term Calendar** — One-glance view of upcoming argument/opinion days and where we are in the term

## Long-Arc Threads

> Threads to track across multiple briefings — cases or doctrines that evolve over many terms.

- **Presidential removal power**: *Myers → Humphrey's Executor → Morrison → Free Enterprise Fund → Seila Law → Collins → Trump v. Slaughter / Trump v. Cook*. **RESOLVED (Jun 29, 2026): *Slaughter* (6–3, Roberts) overruled *Humphrey's Executor*; independent-agency for-cause protection is unconstitutional. *Cook* (5–4, Kavanaugh the fifth vote) carved out the Federal Reserve as constitutionally distinct.** The entire post-New Deal administrative state is now subject to presidential removal at will except the Fed. Next thread to watch: how aggressively the executive uses the new authority, litigation over the Fed line's stability, and the emerging fight over deciding such questions on the emergency docket (live July 2026 — see shadow-docket thread).
- **Birthright citizenship**: *Wong Kim Ark* (1898) → ***Trump v. Barbara* (2026)**. **RESOLVED (Jun 30, 2026): 6–3 to strike EO 14160; 5–4 on the constitutional ground (Roberts + Sotomayor/Kagan/Barrett/Jackson) — "subject to the jurisdiction" reaffirmed per *Wong Kim Ark*.** Kavanaugh concurred on statutory grounds only (§1401(a)), the narrower fallback under which Congress could legislate exceptions. Thomas (91 pp., w/ Gorsuch, Alito) pressed the domicile/allegiance theory. Next to watch: whether Trump's promised legislation materializes and how the statutory-vs-constitutional fault line (Kavanaugh's concurrence) is litigated.
- **Voting rights erosion**: *Crawford → Shelby County → Brnovich → Allen v. Milligan 2023 → Louisiana v. Callais → Allen v. Milligan 2026*. A clear and accelerating trajectory on the redistricting/VRA front (deep dives Jun 23 + Jun 26). **BUT — countertrend (Jun 29): *Watson v. RNC* (5–4, Barrett + Roberts + liberals) UPHELD Mississippi's mail-ballot grace period, an RNC loss preserving grace periods in 29 states + DC.** The erosion is real on race/redistricting but not monolithic on ballot-access mechanics — worth holding both. *Moore v. Harper* / ISL theory queued as retrospective context.
- **Money in politics / campaign finance**: *Buckley v. Valeo* (1976) → *Citizens United* (2010) → *McCutcheon* (2014) → ***NRSC v. FEC* (2026, overruling *Colorado II* (2001))**. A decades-long narrowing of the cognizable "corruption" interest down to *quid pro quo* alone; coordinated party-expenditure limits are the latest to fall. **Deep dive covered Jul 7, 2026** (companion to the Jul 3 *NRSC* spotlight). Watch the 2026 midterms for whether Kagan's circumvention warning bears out.
- **Administrative state rollback**: *Chevron* overruled (*Loper Bright*, 2024) → major questions doctrine (deep dive Jun 19) → *Learning Resources v. Trump* (IEEPA limits) → **removal power capstone: *Slaughter* (*Humphrey's* overruled, Jun 29)**. A multi-term thread now at its separation-of-powers terminus. Remaining frontier: non-delegation revival.
- **LGBTQ First Amendment / Equal Protection**: *Chiles v. Salazar* (2026) established strict scrutiny for viewpoint-based licensing restrictions on talk therapy. **Transgender athlete cases (*Little v. Hecox*, *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*) decided Jun 30, 2026: bans UPHELD — 6–3 on Equal Protection (intermediate scrutiny satisfied), 9–0 no Title IX violation (Kavanaugh).** The Court did NOT declare transgender status a suspect class and left broader trans-rights questions open; Title IX's express athletics provision distinguished *Bostock*. Doctrinal fault lines covered in the Jun 30 *Bostock* dive (textual method) and the Jul 3 *VMI* dive (scrutiny tier / overbreadth). Next to watch: how lower courts read *Hecox*'s "no suspect class" silence into other trans-rights contexts (bathrooms, healthcare, ID) where no physiological-difference rationale applies.
- **Immigration / executive latitude at the border**: *Mullin v. Al Otro Lado* (turn-back / "arrival") and *Mullin v. Doe* (TPS termination) both decided **June 25 for the administration** (Alito), alongside *Blanche v. Lau* (Jun 23, returning-LPR admissions). A consistent pattern of deference to executive immigration enforcement this term — with *Trump v. Barbara* (Jun 30) the notable exception, a rare loss on the birthright question. Next deep-dive hooks: plenary power doctrine; TPS mechanics.
- **Second Amendment after *Bruen***: *Bruen* (2022) → *Rahimi* (2024) → *Hemani* (2026, §922(g)(3) drug-user prohibition) → *Wolford v. Lopez* (2026, public carry on private property open to the public). The history-and-tradition test is now reshaping both prohibited-possessor categories and the geography of carry. Watch how §922(g)(1) (felon-in-possession) fares next.
- **Digital privacy / Fourth Amendment**: *Katz* (1967) → third-party doctrine (*Smith v. Maryland*) → *Carpenter* (2018, cell-site location) → ***Chatrie v. United States* (2026, geofence warrants are a "search")**. A cross-ideological line (Kagan + Roberts + Gorsuch) continuing *Carpenter*'s erosion of the third-party doctrine for digital location data. Civic-tech relevance.
- **The emergency ("shadow") docket** *(new thread, Jul 3, 2026)*: The post-*Cook* fallout has turned the emergency docket itself into a contested subject — three conservative dissenters criticized the *Cook* majority for deciding a consequential removal question through emergency procedure and "short-circuiting" lower courts; Roberts defended it as a matter of "prudence." A live July 2026 story and a natural recess Deep Dive (queued under Executive Power). **Data point (surfaced Jul 7 briefing): in 2026 the Court issued more emergency-docket orders (63) than merits opinions (56)** — the center of gravity has formally shifted. Recent recess orders: *Blanche v. Perlmutter* (June 30, stay DENIED — Register of Copyrights stays; a rare administration emergency loss) and *Herridge v. Chen* (July 2, stay denied, Kavanaugh would grant — reporter's privilege). Watch whether the internal criticism produces any procedural reform or greater opinion-writing on emergency orders.
