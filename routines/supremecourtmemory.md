# Supreme Court Memory File

> This file is maintained by the bi-weekly SCOTUS briefing system. It tracks deep dive topics already covered, significant cases from the current term, and long-arc precedent threads — so that each briefing builds on prior ones rather than repeating them.

---

## Purpose

Evie follows the Supreme Court as her primary news stream — because unlike most news, the Court's work is foundational rather than episodic. A case decided today may constrain legislation for a generation. Her orientation is that of a **liberally-inclined originalist**: accepting the originalist critique of judicial overreach while holding liberal political commitments, and believing the Court's legitimacy depends on its restraint, not its outcomes. The briefing serves both intellectual depth and practical awareness — particularly for her roles on the WA LGBTQ Commission and in civic tech policy.

---

## Briefing Format (Reference)

Each bi-weekly briefing contains:

1. **Status Snapshot** — Opinions released since last briefing; emergency applications and notable orders; argument calendar for the next two weeks
2. **Case Spotlight** — 1–2 pending cases examined in moderate depth (parties, question presented, circuit split or statutory hook, oral argument tea leaves if available)
3. **Deep Dive** — One topic per issue: a landmark precedent, a doctrinal thread, a constitutional concept, or a Court history item that broadens the context window for cases on the docket. These should NOT repeat. See tracking below.
4. **Term Calendar** — One-glance view of upcoming argument/opinion days and where we are in the term

## Long-Arc Threads

> Threads to track across multiple briefings — cases or doctrines that evolve over many terms.

- **Presidential removal power**: *Myers → Humphrey's Executor → Morrison → Free Enterprise Fund → Seila Law → Collins → Trump v. Cook / Trump v. Slaughter*. Will extend into future terms.
- **Birthright citizenship**: *Wong Kim Ark* (1898) is the only SCOTUS precedent directly on point. *Trump v. Barbara* may be the most significant 14th Amendment case in a generation.
- **Voting rights erosion**: *Crawford → Shelby County → Brnovich → Allen v. Milligan 2023 → Allen v. Milligan 2026*. A clear trajectory.
- **Administrative state rollback**: *Chevron* overruled (*Loper Bright*, 2024) → major questions doctrine → non-delegation revival? A multi-term thread with enormous downstream consequences.
